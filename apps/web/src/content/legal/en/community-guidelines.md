## 1. What these guidelines cover

1.1. These guidelines apply to everything people add to the Service and show to someone else: reviews and trainer replies, showcases, story cards, challenge entries and nicknames, comments on food diary days, notes, messages sent through the bot, feedback and reports. They form part of the [Terms of Service](terms-of-service.md).

1.2. The Service is run by [OPERATOR FULL NAME], an individual, at [ADDRESS] ("DaFit", "we", "us"). Words such as Service, user, trainer, client, pair, assistant and showcase have the meaning given in the [Terms of Service](terms-of-service.md), section 2.

1.3. Most of what happens in DaFit is private to a pair. These rules still apply there: a trainer's comment on a client's food diary must be as respectful as a public post.

## 2. Content that is not allowed

Do not post, upload or send:

2.1. anything illegal where you or the recipient are, including content that infringes copyright or trademarks;

2.2. threats, harassment, bullying, or content that attacks people for their body, weight, age, sex, gender, sexual orientation, ethnicity, religion, disability or health;

2.3. sexual content, nudity, or sexualised comments about anyone. Progress photos are for tracking progress only (front, side and back views, as the app offers);

2.4. photos or personal data of another person without their permission, including screenshots of someone else's data in DaFit;

2.5. content that encourages eating disorders, self-harm, dangerous fasting, or the use of illegal or unprescribed drugs, steroids or other performance-enhancing substances;

2.6. medical claims: promises to cure, treat or diagnose a condition, or advice to stop medical treatment (see the [Health Disclaimer](health-disclaimer.md));

2.7. false or misleading claims about qualifications, results or prices, including on a showcase;

2.8. spam, chain messages, or advertising for anything other than your own coaching services on your showcase;

2.9. malware, phishing links, or links that collect people's Telegram or payment details;

2.10. impersonation of another trainer, client, DaFit or Telegram.

## 3. Reviews

### 3.1. Who can write a review

3.1.1. Only a client of a pair can review that pair's trainer, and only after at least **three sessions that took place and that the client attended**. DaFit checks this from the schedule before it lets anyone write a review. This is how we make sure reviews come from people who actually trained with the trainer.

3.1.2. One review per pair. The author can edit it or delete it at any time. When a review is edited, DaFit keeps the earlier version for the moderation team, so that a dispute can be judged fairly. When the author deletes a review, it disappears for the trainer, and the team keeps a copy for the same reason. Earlier versions and deleted reviews are erased when the author's account is deleted.

3.1.3. A review is a rating from 1 to 5 and optional text, up to 2,000 characters.

### 3.2. What a review should be

3.2.1. Your own honest experience with this trainer. Talk about the sessions, not about the trainer's private life.

3.2.2. No content from section 2, no personal data of others, no contact details, no links.

### 3.3. No fake or paid-for reviews

3.3.1. Do not write a review for a trainer you have not trained with, about yourself, or for someone else's account.

3.3.2. Trainers must not write, buy or ask anyone to write reviews of themselves or of competitors, and must not ask family, friends or staff to review them without saying so in the review.

3.3.3. Trainers must not offer anything in return for a review (a discount, a free session, a gift, a prize in a challenge), and must never make a reward depend on a positive review. A trainer can ask a client for an honest review, the same way for every client.

3.3.4. Trainers must not pressure, threaten or punish clients over a review, or ask them to change or remove it, except to point out a real factual mistake.

3.3.5. Breaking these rules is unlawful in many countries, for example under the UK Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (since 6 April 2025), the US FTC rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, since 21 October 2024) and the EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (Annex I, points 23b and 23c). We remove such reviews and may close the accounts involved.

### 3.4. Who sees reviews

3.4.1. Today a review is seen only by its author, the trainer (unless we hid it, see 3.6), and the DaFit team.

3.4.2. **We will not publish a review without its author's consent.** If we later show reviews on public pages, such as the showcase or a trainer catalogue, we will ask each author first. Reviews whose author did not agree stay private. An author can withdraw consent at any time, and the review then stops being public.

3.4.3. When reviews are public, we will show all published reviews of a trainer whose authors agreed, including negative ones, ordered by date unless the page says otherwise. The rating shown is the plain average of those reviews. We do not accept payment to show, rank, hide or remove reviews.

> Counsel: UCPD Art. 7(6) (added by the Omnibus Directive 2019/2161) requires a trader that gives access to consumer reviews to say whether and how it ensures that reviews come from consumers who used the product. Sections 3.1.1 and 3.4.3 are that statement; they must be shown, or linked, where reviews are displayed. Check that the final averaging and ordering match the build.

### 3.5. Trainer replies

3.5.1. A trainer can post one reply to each review and edit it. The reply is shown with the review to the same people. An empty reply removes it.

3.5.2. Replies follow the same rules as reviews. Reply to the content, do not attack the author, and do not reveal anything about the client the review itself does not mention, including health information or what you know from your sessions.

### 3.6. Disputing a review

3.6.1. A trainer who thinks a review breaks these guidelines can press "Dispute" and give a reason. The review goes to our moderation queue. Only one dispute per review can be open at a time.

3.6.2. A person on the DaFit team looks at it and either keeps the review or hides it with a reason. If it is hidden, the trainer no longer sees it and the author sees the reason. Both sides can appeal (section 8).

3.6.3. We do not hide a review just because it is negative.

## 4. Showcases

4.1. A showcase is public. Everything on it must be accurate: name, photo, qualifications, prices, locations and offers.

4.2. Use only photos you have the right to use, and show other people only with their permission.

4.3. If a trainer's verification is rejected, the showcase goes offline until the trainer is verified.

## 5. Challenges and story cards

5.1. Challenge leaderboards use nicknames. Pick a nickname that does not break section 2 and does not impersonate anyone.

5.2. Story cards show only what you chose. Check the card before sharing; anyone with the link can see it for 30 days.

## 6. How we moderate

6.1. We do not check content before it appears. We act on reports, on disputes, and on what our team comes across while running the Service.

6.2. Decisions are made by people on the DaFit team. We do not use automated tools to remove content or restrict accounts today. If that changes, we will say so here and in every statement of reasons.

6.3. Depending on how serious a problem is, we may: ask you to change something; hide or remove content; take a showcase offline; limit features such as reviews or challenges; suspend an account for a set time; or close it.

6.4. Our team cannot read clients' nutrition, measurements, photos or questionnaires in normal operation. If a report concerns such content, we may use the time-limited emergency access described in the [Terms of Service](terms-of-service.md), section 13.3, with a recorded reason, and the person concerned is notified unless the law forbids it.

- why you think the content is illegal or breaks these guidelines;
- where it is: a link to the showcase or a description precise enough for us to find it (for example the trainer, the date and the first words of a review);

7.4. **Threats to life or safety.** If a report or content gives us reason to suspect a criminal offence that threatens someone's life or safety, we inform the police or other competent authorities straight away.

## 8. Statement of reasons and appeals

8.1. When we remove, hide or restrict your content or account, we send you through the bot or in the app a statement of reasons with: what we did and its scope and duration; the facts behind it; whether we acted on a report or on our own initiative; the law or the rule in these guidelines or the [Terms of Service](terms-of-service.md) that we relied on; and how you can appeal.

> Team: not in the product yet, see product-changes. The app does not yet send a statement of reasons. When a review is hidden, only the author sees the reason.

8.2. **Appeal.** You, or the person who reported the content, can appeal within 6 months by writing to [CONTACT EMAIL]. Someone who did not take the first decision reviews the appeal. We reverse our decision without delay if the appeal shows we were wrong.

8.3. If you live in the EU, you can also take the dispute to an out-of-court dispute settlement body certified under Article 21 of the DSA, or go to court. The European Commission publishes the list of certified bodies.

## 9. Repeat offenders and misuse of reports

9.1. **Repeat offenders.** If an account repeatedly posts content that is clearly illegal, or repeatedly breaks these guidelines after warnings, we first suspend it for a reasonable period after a warning, and close it if it continues. For copyright, we close accounts of repeat infringers in appropriate circumstances.

9.2. **Abuse of reports.** If someone often sends reports or disputes that are clearly unfounded, we may stop processing their reports for a reasonable period after a warning.

9.3. When we decide, we look at the number of cases, how serious they were, how recent, and whether the person acted on purpose.
